Reference bevel.css 0.4.0

Every class, variant and state, with the markup to copy.

Link bevel.css before your own stylesheet. It follows the system’s light or dark setting; set data-theme="light" or data-theme="dark" on <html> to force one. Everything in it sits in @layer bevel.*, so your CSS, written outside any layer, overrides it without !important. States take the class or the ARIA attribute: .active or aria-current, .disabled or aria-disabled, .error or aria-invalid.

Typography

A heading sits 9px above what it introduces and 18px below whatever came before it, so it always reads with the block under it. At the top of a column, alert or well it gets no space above.

An h2 is a section label: small capitals, tracked out, with 36px above it. When it holds only text and a <small>, a dotted leader runs from the label to the <small> at the end of the line, for a count or a date.

Body text

The lead paragraph: 18px on a 28px line, for the one sentence that sets up a page.

Body text is 14px on a 20px line, in Helvetica Neue, or Helvetica or Arial where it isn’t installed. It carries links, strong words, small text that keeps the surrounding size and inline code such as hearth deploy recipes.

A longer paragraph wraps at about 70 characters a line, however wide the page. Each app keeps a small manifest next to its code, and hearth reads it to decide how to pull, build and run the app, which port it listens on, and whether it should come back after the machine restarts. Everything else has a sensible default, so most manifests are four or five lines long.

Navbar

Links on the left, the brand on the right. Mark the current page with aria-current="page" (as in the bar at the top of this page) or .active (below); it turns white and glows, on a tab sunk into the bar. Below 768px the links wrap onto more rows and the divider hides.

Add .navbar-sticky to keep the bar at the top while the page scrolls under it, as the bar on this page does. Below 768px it scrolls away with the page.

Put a .skip-link first in the page, pointing at <main id="main">. It stays off-screen until it takes keyboard focus, so keyboard users can jump past the navbar: press Tab at the top of this page to see it.

A switch in the bar

A <button> in a .nav looks like a link, for an action such as switching between light and dark, and its content is centered in the bar. Wrap a field and a .add-on in .input-prepend or .input-append to put a unit or a fixed part of the value beside it. A screen reader doesn’t read the add-on as part of the label, so give it an id and add that to the field’s aria-describedby.

Progress bars

A .progress track holds a .bar; set the bar’s width inline. The track is faint on white, so say the value in words too, above the bar or elsewhere in its table row. Give the track role="progressbar" for work under way, such as a backup, or role="meter" for a level, such as disk space or a budget, with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax and a name.

Utilities

.muted sets text in the muted grey, for asides and timestamps. It keeps 4.5:1 on white and on bevel’s grey surfaces: striped and hovered table rows, the hero unit and the form actions bar.

.text-right aligns to the end of the line, for numbers and trailing actions. On a paragraph it also lifts the line-length cap, so the text reaches the end.

.table-scroll wraps a wide table; see Tables.

.visually-hidden keeps text for screen readers and hides it from view: a label the page already makes clear, or the words for an icon-only button. The search field and the buttons below each have one.

<label class="visually-hidden" for="filter">Filter apps</label>
